The book and its inspiration

I Refuse to Stay Bound

Overcoming Offense, Releasing Bitterness, and Walking in the Freedom of Jesus Christ

A meaningful inspiration for this book is The Bait of Satan by John Bevere, a work that examines the danger of offense and the way it can affect our spiritual lives. That message helped inspire further reflection on offense, bitterness, forgiveness, and the freedom found in Jesus Christ.

I Refuse to Stay Bound is Bradley Hobbs’s own work, written in his own voice. Its teaching, reflections, prayers, affirmations, and practical application are woven naturally throughout the message, inviting readers to consider what they carry and how they can move forward in Christ.

What does it mean to refuse to stay bound?

I Refuse to Stay Bound: Overcoming Offense, Releasing Bitterness, and Walking in the Freedom of Jesus Christ is a Christian book by Bradley Hobbs about the spiritual and personal work of facing offense, confronting bitterness, practicing forgiveness, and pursuing freedom through Jesus Christ.

Being hurt is not the same as being defined by hurt. Forgiveness does not require pretending that harm never happened, and grace does not mean abandoning discernment or healthy boundaries. The journey calls for honesty, prayer, biblical reflection, and a willingness to let God reshape the way we respond.

Who is this book for?

This message is for readers who have struggled with painful relationships, unresolved offense, disappointment, church hurt, resentment, or the burden of carrying yesterday’s wounds into today. It is also intended to support personal devotion, group discussion, and continued spiritual growth.

One connected message. Teaching, reflections, prayers, affirmations, and practical application are part of the book itself, woven into the writing rather than offered as separate companion materials. Publication details will be shared when finalized.

The heart of the journey

The goal is not to minimize pain or rush someone through healing. It is to encourage a Christ-centered process of truth, forgiveness, wisdom, prayer, and freedom, one step at a time.
