Chapter 14 Study Guide

The Courage to Trust Again

Home›Inside the Chapters›Chapter 14›Study Guide
Group Study Guide

Group Discussion Questions

1. Trust and discernment

Read Matthew 10:16. How do wisdom and innocence work together, and why is it important that neither quality replaces the other?

2. Guarding the heart

Read Proverbs 4:23. What is the difference between protecting your heart responsibly and closing yourself off from every meaningful relationship?

3. Evaluating character

Read John 2:23–25 and Matthew 7:16–20. What can consistent conduct reveal that words, promises, and public appearances may not reveal?

4. The influence of past wounds

How can a painful experience affect the way someone interprets a new relationship? What practical steps can help distinguish present evidence from fear rooted in the past?

5. Forgiveness and renewed trust

Why does forgiving someone not automatically require restoring the same relationship or giving that person the same access they previously had?

6. Trustworthy leadership

What practices help make a congregation or spiritual leader worthy of trust? Consider accountability, safeguarding, transparency, appropriate confidentiality, fair processes, and the way leaders respond to correction.

7. Personal application

Identify one small, responsible step that could help you remain open to healthy connection while maintaining appropriate boundaries. What support or wisdom would help you take that step?

Personal Application

Take time to consider how betrayal or disappointment may have influenced your expectations of other people. Have you become more discerning in ways that protect your well-being, or have you begun to assume that every new relationship will eventually repeat an old wound? Are there people who have demonstrated reliability whom you continue to hold at a distance because trusting anyone feels dangerous? Conversely, are there relationships in which you have felt pressured to extend trust before the other person has demonstrated the character necessary to sustain it?

Approach these questions without condemning yourself. The purpose of reflection is not to blame you for the way you responded to pain, but to help you recognize where a once-useful protective response may need to change. You are allowed to learn from what happened without allowing it to govern every future decision.

Ask God for wisdom to recognize trustworthy people, courage to establish necessary boundaries, and humility to acknowledge when fear may be influencing your interpretation of a situation. If the effects of betrayal remain overwhelming, consider seeking support from a qualified counselor or another trustworthy person who can help you work through those experiences at a responsible pace.

During the coming week, choose one relationship or situation in which you want to practice wise trust. This does not mean exposing yourself to someone who has harmed you or removing a boundary that currently protects your safety. Instead, consider whether there is a small, appropriate step you can take toward a healthy relationship, such as accepting a reasonable invitation, having an honest conversation, asking a clarifying question rather than assuming someone’s motives, or allowing a trustworthy person to offer support.

As you take that step, pay attention to the other person’s conduct and to your own responses. If concerns arise, examine them thoughtfully rather than ignoring them or immediately assuming the worst. If someone repeatedly disregards your boundaries or demonstrates harmful behavior, take that evidence seriously and respond appropriately.

Write this statement somewhere you can revisit it during the week:

I will not allow the person who hurt me to determine how I treat everyone I meet. I will practice love with discernment, extend trust responsibly, and allow character to speak through consistent actions.

Closing Prayer

Lord Jesus, teach me how to trust wisely again. You know the experiences that have made my heart cautious, the disappointments that have weakened my confidence, and the memories that sometimes make new relationships feel more frightening than hopeful. I bring those experiences before You without pretending that they did not affect me, and I ask You to help me heal without allowing fear to govern the rest of my life.

Give me discernment to recognize honesty, humility, consistency, and genuine care. Help me notice warning signs without becoming suspicious of everyone, and teach me to establish boundaries without using them to punish people who have not harmed me. When I am tempted to assume that the past must repeat itself, help me examine the present with wisdom. When I am tempted to ignore a legitimate concern because I want a relationship to succeed, give me the courage to respond truthfully.

Help me forgive without surrendering responsibility for my well-being. Teach me to understand that reconciliation requires more than one person’s willingness and that trust must be supported by conduct. Where a relationship can be restored safely and honestly, give all involved the humility to take responsibility and make meaningful changes. Where a relationship cannot responsibly continue, help me release the desire for revenge and move forward with wisdom.

I pray for those who have been wounded within the Church. Comfort those who are grieving, protect those who remain vulnerable, and guide those who are trying to find healthy fellowship again. Give leaders the courage to accept accountability, listen to difficult concerns, and demonstrate the character of Christ in their conduct. Let congregations become places where truth is welcomed, boundaries are respected, and people are treated with dignity.

Guard my heart without hardening it. Keep my compassion alive without allowing me to confuse love with unlimited access. Help me recognize the difference between caution and fear, between forgiveness and restored trust, and between healthy correction and harmful control. Give me patience to let relationships develop responsibly and the courage to step away when evidence shows that a relationship is not healthy.

Above all, remind me that my future does not have to be controlled by my past. Help me become wiser through what I have experienced without becoming bitter toward the people I have not yet met. Lead me into relationships that encourage truth, growth, mutual respect, and faithful service to You. Let my life demonstrate that healing can produce discernment without destroying compassion.

In the name of Jesus, Amen.
