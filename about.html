The book and its inspiration

I Refuse to Stay Bound

Overcoming Offense, Releasing Bitterness, and Walking in the Freedom of Jesus Christ

A meaningful inspiration for this book is The Bait of Satan by John Bevere, which examines the danger of offense and the ways it can affect our spiritual lives. That message prompted further reflection on forgiveness, bitterness, betrayal, and the freedom found in Jesus Christ.

I Refuse to Stay Bound is Bradley Hobbs's own work, written in his own voice and shaped around his own teaching and message. It invites readers to consider what they carry, examine their responses in the light of Scripture, and choose a path toward spiritual growth and freedom.

What Does It Mean to Refuse to Stay Bound?

I Refuse to Stay Bound is a Christian teaching book about facing offense, confronting bitterness, practicing forgiveness, and pursuing freedom through Jesus Christ. It challenges readers to look honestly at the heart, consider how unresolved hurt can influence their responses, and allow the truth of God's Word to shape the way they live.

Being hurt is not the same as being defined by hurt. Forgiveness does not require pretending that harm never happened, and grace does not mean abandoning discernment or healthy boundaries. A faithful response calls for honesty, prayer, biblical understanding, accountability, and a willingness to let God reshape the way we respond.

Who Is This Book For?

This message is for readers who have struggled with painful relationships, unresolved offense, disappointment, church hurt, resentment, betrayal, or the burden of carrying yesterday's wounds into today. It is also intended to support personal devotion, church discussion, small group study, and continued spiritual growth.

For Personal Reflection

Read at your own pace and give yourself room to consider the teaching, Scripture, reflections, prayers, and affirmations already woven throughout the book.

For Churches and Groups

Use the book as a starting point for thoughtful conversations about offense, forgiveness, repentance, accountability, and the freedom found in Jesus Christ.

For Those Seeking Clarity

Consider the difference between forgiveness and reconciliation, grace and enabling, and pursuing peace while maintaining wise and appropriate boundaries.

For Continued Spiritual Growth

Return to Scripture and the message of the book as you seek to respond with greater wisdom, humility, truth, and Christlike character.

One Connected Message

The teaching, reflections, prayers, affirmations, and practical application belong within the chapters themselves. They are part of the reading experience, not separate materials that repeat the book. The website's Resources page provides additional Scripture references, a seven day reading journey, guidance for church and small group study, and suggestions for further exploration.

More Than Reading

The purpose is not to rush someone through a painful experience or minimize what happened. It is to encourage an honest, Christ centered process of truth, forgiveness, wisdom, prayer, accountability, and freedom. Each reader can take time to consider the message and apply it thoughtfully in daily life.

Forgiveness should never be used to silence someone who has been harmed, excuse wrongdoing, or prevent appropriate boundaries. Healing and reconciliation are not always the same process, and rebuilding trust may require repentance, consistent change, and time.

The Heart of the Journey

Through Jesus Christ, we can seek truth, choose forgiveness, practice wisdom, and continue growing in a life shaped by God's Word.
