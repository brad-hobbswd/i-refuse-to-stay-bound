12 Week Syllabus and Curriculum

I Refuse to Stay Bound is a Scripture centered study on overcoming offense, releasing bitterness, practicing forgiveness with wisdom, and walking in the freedom of Jesus Christ. This twelve week plan provides a ready to use framework for churches, small groups, ministry teams, and personal study.

Course Syllabus

Course length: 12 weeks, one guided session per week.

Course purpose: To help participants examine their own hearts through Scripture, recognize how offense and bitterness can shape responses, and practice forgiveness, accountability, wise boundaries, and spiritual growth in Jesus Christ.

Required resource: I Refuse to Stay Bound by Bradley Hobbs. Participants should read the assigned portion of the book before each session and bring a Bible and a notebook.

Learning Outcomes

  • Identify personal responses to hurt without denying that harm occurred.
  • Study biblical teaching about offense, forgiveness, mercy, repentance, and accountability.
  • Distinguish forgiveness from restored trust and reconciliation.
  • Recognize the importance of healthy boundaries, safety, and wise counsel.
  • Apply Scripture to personal choices, attitudes, words, and relationships.
  • Develop a practical plan for continuing to walk in the freedom of Jesus Christ.

Suggested Weekly Session Format

  1. Opening prayer and review of the previous week's personal application.
  2. Read the assigned book section and the week's Scripture passages in context.
  3. Teach and discuss the central theme, allowing space for thoughtful questions.
  4. Complete the personal reflection and practical application.
  5. Close with prayer. Sharing personal experiences is always voluntary.

Leader guidance: Keep discussion focused on Scripture and personal application. Do not pressure participants to disclose painful experiences, name other people, or reconcile in situations where safety and accountability have not been established.

Twelve Week Study Plan

Read the corresponding chapter or chapters in the book each week, following the book's existing order. The weekly themes below organize the course without replacing or renaming the book's chapter titles.

WEEK 01

The Decision to Walk in Freedom

Focus: Begin with willingness, humility, and an honest examination of the heart.

Scripture: Psalm 139:23–24; John 8:31–32.

Personal application: Write down one area where you want God's truth to shape your response.

WEEK 02

Recognizing Offense

Focus: Understand how hurt and offense can influence thoughts, words, decisions, and relationships.

Scripture: Proverbs 18:19; Hebrews 12:14–15.

Personal application: Notice your own responses to hurt without assigning blame to others during the exercise.

WEEK 03

Examining the Heart

Focus: Invite correction and distinguish self examination from condemnation.

Scripture: Psalm 51:10; James 1:19–20.

Personal application: Identify one attitude, habit, or response that needs prayerful attention.

WEEK 04

The Weight of Bitterness

Focus: Consider how resentment can take root and affect spiritual and relational life.

Scripture: Ephesians 4:31–32; Hebrews 12:15.

Personal application: Name privately what you need to bring honestly before Jesus in prayer.

WEEK 05

Choosing Forgiveness

Focus: Study forgiveness as a deliberate response to the teaching of Jesus, not as denial of wrongdoing.

Scripture: Matthew 6:14–15; Colossians 3:12–13.

Personal application: Write a personal prayer asking Jesus to help you take the next honest step toward forgiveness.

WEEK 06

Forgiveness, Repentance, and Accountability

Focus: Explore the relationship between mercy, changed conduct, responsibility, and truth.

Scripture: Luke 17:3–4; 2 Corinthians 7:9–11.

Personal application: Consider where accountability or a change in your own conduct may be needed.

WEEK 07

Forgiveness and Reconciliation

Focus: Recognize that forgiveness and reconciliation are related but not identical.

Scripture: Romans 12:18; Matthew 5:23–24.

Personal application: Identify what is within your responsibility and what requires another person's willing participation.

WEEK 08

Trust, Wisdom, and Boundaries

Focus: Consider how forgiveness can coexist with discernment, safety, and appropriate boundaries.

Scripture: Proverbs 4:23; Proverbs 22:3.

Personal application: Write one wise boundary or support step that may help protect peace and well being.

WEEK 09

Responding Without Retaliation

Focus: Learn to resist revenge and allow Scripture to guide a faithful response to conflict.

Scripture: Romans 12:17–21; 1 Peter 3:9.

Personal application: Plan a calm, truthful, and responsible response to a difficult situation.

WEEK 10

Walking in Truth and Spiritual Freedom

Focus: Connect freedom in Jesus Christ with continuing obedience to His Word.

Scripture: John 8:31–36; Galatians 5:1.

Personal application: Choose one truth from Scripture to remember and practice during the week.

WEEK 11

Living Out What You Have Learned

Focus: Apply the study to daily choices, communication, relationships, and spiritual maturity.

Scripture: Ephesians 4:15; James 1:22–25.

Personal application: Review your notes and identify a specific change you can continue practicing.

WEEK 12

Continuing in Freedom

Focus: Review the course, give thanks for growth, and prepare for continued personal application.

Scripture: Philippians 3:13–14; 2 Corinthians 3:17.

Personal application: Create a personal plan for prayer, Scripture reading, accountability, and ongoing growth.

Course Completion Reflection

At the final session, invite each participant to complete a personal reflection: What truth has challenged me most? What have I learned about forgiveness and responsibility? What boundary, habit, or response do I need to continue addressing? What will help me remain grounded in Scripture and keep walking in the freedom of Jesus Christ?

Completion can be recognized through participation, weekly personal application, and a written reflection. No participant should be required to disclose private or traumatic experiences to complete the course.

Scripture Reference Guide

Scripture gives us a foundation for examining our hearts, understanding our responsibilities, and seeking the wisdom of God. Use the subjects below to explore passages connected to the central themes of the book.

These references are starting points for personal study. Read each passage in its surrounding context, paying attention to whom it addresses and what it teaches.

Forgiveness and Mercy
  • Matthew 6:14–15 examines forgiveness in the life of a disciple.
  • Matthew 18:21–35 presents Jesus' teaching about forgiveness and mercy.
  • Ephesians 4:31–32 calls believers away from bitterness and toward kindness, compassion, and forgiveness.
  • Colossians 3:12–13 connects compassion, patience, and forgiveness with the life of a believer.

Consider how receiving mercy from God should influence the way you respond to people who have hurt you.

Offense, Anger, and Bitterness
  • Hebrews 12:14–15 warns against allowing a root of bitterness to grow.
  • James 1:19–20 teaches believers to be quick to hear, slow to speak, and slow to anger.
  • Proverbs 19:11 considers patience and the wisdom of overlooking certain offenses.
  • Ephesians 4:26–27 addresses anger and warns against giving the devil an opportunity.

Examine the difference between acknowledging that something was wrong and allowing resentment to govern your decisions.

Repentance and Examination of the Heart
  • Psalm 139:23–24 expresses a desire for God to search the heart and lead in the right way.
  • Psalm 51:10 expresses the desire for a clean heart and a renewed spirit.
  • 2 Corinthians 13:5 calls believers to examine themselves.
  • 1 John 1:9 addresses confession and God's faithfulness to forgive and cleanse.

Ask God to reveal not only the wounds you carry but also the attitudes, words, and actions that require your own repentance.

Reconciliation, Repentance, and Accountability
  • Matthew 5:23–24 emphasizes taking responsibility for reconciliation with a brother or sister.
  • Matthew 18:15–17 describes a process for addressing wrongdoing within the community of believers.
  • Luke 17:3–4 addresses correction, repentance, and forgiveness.
  • 2 Corinthians 7:9–11 discusses godly sorrow and the fruit of repentance.

Consider the difference between offering forgiveness and rebuilding a relationship. Reconciliation requires wisdom, and meaningful restoration may involve repentance, accountability, and changed behavior.

Wisdom, Boundaries, and Discernment
  • Proverbs 4:23 emphasizes guarding the heart because life flows from it.
  • Proverbs 22:3 considers the wisdom of recognizing danger and responding prudently.
  • Matthew 10:16 calls for both shrewdness and innocence.
  • Romans 12:18 instructs believers to live peaceably with others as far as it depends on them.

Consider how to pursue peace without denying reality, abandoning wisdom, or permitting continued harm.

Freedom in Jesus Christ
  • John 8:31–36 connects abiding in Jesus' word with truth and freedom.
  • 2 Corinthians 3:17 speaks of the Spirit of the Lord and liberty.
  • Galatians 5:1 calls believers to stand firm in the freedom Christ provides.
  • Romans 8:1–2 addresses freedom from condemnation and the law of the Spirit of life in Christ Jesus.

Reflect on the difference between knowing biblical truth intellectually and allowing that truth to shape your daily walk with Jesus.

Love, Truth, and Christian Character
  • 1 Corinthians 13:4–7 describes the character and conduct of love.
  • Ephesians 4:15 speaks of speaking the truth in love.
  • Micah 6:8 emphasizes justice, mercy, and walking humbly with God.
  • Romans 12:17–21 teaches believers not to repay evil for evil and calls them to overcome evil with good.

Examine whether your responses reflect both the truth of God's Word and the character of Jesus Christ.

A Note on Scripture Study

A single verse should not be used to silence someone who has been harmed or to excuse wrongdoing. Read passages in context and consider the full biblical teaching on truth, mercy, justice, repentance, and responsibility.

A Seven Day Reading Journey

This seven day reading journey invites you to slow down, engage with the message of I Refuse to Stay Bound, and consider how biblical truth applies to your daily walk with Jesus Christ.

Rather than rushing through the book, approach each day with an open heart. Read the assigned portion thoughtfully, examine the Scriptures referenced in the text, and allow yourself time to consider what you are learning.

Read the chapters in their published order. Divide the 27 chapters across the seven days according to their length. Some days may require more reading than others. This guide supplements the book and does not replace its existing teaching, reflections, prayers, or affirmations.

DAY01

Begin with an Open Heart

Begin with the introduction and the opening portion of the book. Approach the message with a willingness to examine your own heart rather than concentrating only on the faults of others.

DAY02

Recognize the Power of Offense

Continue reading in the established chapter order. Consider how unresolved hurt, resentment, and personal responses can influence your thoughts, decisions, and relationships.

DAY03

Examine Your Own Heart

Continue through the next portion of the book. Be willing to recognize attitudes, words, and choices that may need correction. Honest self examination requires humility and a willingness to respond to the truth of God's Word.

DAY04

Understand Forgiveness and Responsibility

Continue reading and pay attention to what the book teaches about forgiveness, repentance, accountability, and reconciliation. Consider why forgiveness should never be used to excuse wrongdoing or prevent someone from establishing appropriate boundaries.

DAY05

Choose Truth Over Bitterness

Continue through the manuscript. Reflect on the difference between acknowledging that you have been hurt and allowing that hurt to govern your life. Consider how the truth of Scripture can guide your responses.

DAY06

Walk in Wisdom and Freedom

Read the next portion of the book, moving toward its conclusion. Consider how your relationship with Jesus Christ should shape your choices, boundaries, relationships, and daily conduct.

DAY07

Reflect, Respond, and Continue

Complete any remaining chapters. Return to the teaching, Scripture references, prayers, and affirmations that spoke to you most clearly. Consider what you have learned and how you can continue applying biblical truth in your daily walk.

Seven Days to Begin. A Lifetime of Growth.

The purpose of this journey is not simply to finish a book in seven days. It is to create space for honest reflection, biblical understanding, and meaningful personal application. If you need additional time, take it. Spiritual growth is not a race.

Let the message move beyond the page and into the way you live, respond, forgive, exercise wisdom, and walk with Jesus Christ.

Church and Small Group Study

I Refuse to Stay Bound may serve as a starting point for conversations about forgiveness, offense, bitterness, accountability, and spiritual freedom. This framework can help pastors, teachers, and group facilitators create thoughtful discussions without duplicating the chapter material.

1. Establish the Purpose

Explain that the goal is to examine Scripture, consider personal responsibility, and grow in the character of Jesus Christ. The group is not a place to identify, accuse, or publicly confront other people.

2. Read the Assigned Chapter

Ask participants to read the selected chapter before meeting when possible. During the session, focus on the chapter's existing teaching and biblical foundations.

3. Examine the Scripture

Read the biblical passages in context. Invite participants to consider what the text actually teaches and how it relates to the subject under discussion.

4. Encourage Honest Reflection

Allow participants to think privately before sharing. Personal disclosure should remain voluntary. No one should be pressured to recount painful experiences or reveal confidential details.

5. Distinguish Forgiveness from Reconciliation

Make room for an honest discussion about repentance, trust, accountability, and safety. Do not suggest that forgiveness requires a person to remain in a harmful situation or immediately restore a relationship.

6. Encourage Practical Application

Invite participants to identify a personal next step related to their own choices, attitudes, or boundaries. Keep the emphasis on personal application rather than trying to control another person's response.

7. Close with Prayer

Allow time for prayer using the prayer already included in the assigned chapter. Respect participants who prefer to pray privately or who are not ready to share aloud.

Guidance for Group Leaders

Protect confidentiality while explaining its limits when safety concerns arise. Do not use forgiveness teaching to silence reports of abuse, discourage appropriate reporting, or shield leaders from accountability. When serious harm is disclosed, respond with care and help the person access appropriate support.

Understanding Biblical Forgiveness and Freedom

Some of the most difficult conversations about forgiveness arise when biblical principles are confused with expectations that Scripture does not require in every circumstance. These distinctions can help readers study the subject carefully.

Forgiveness and Reconciliation

Forgiveness and reconciliation are related, but they are not identical. Reconciliation concerns the restoration of a relationship. Rebuilding a damaged relationship may require honesty, repentance, accountability, and time.

Study: Matthew 18:15–17 and Romans 12:18.

Forgiveness and Trust

Forgiveness does not automatically establish that another person is trustworthy. Trust develops through demonstrated honesty, responsibility, consistency, and changed behavior.

Study: Proverbs 22:3 and Luke 17:3–4.

Grace and Accountability

Grace is not permission to continue wrongdoing. Biblical mercy must not become an excuse to avoid repentance or the consequences of harmful choices.

Study: 2 Corinthians 7:9–11 and Galatians 6:1.

Peace and Boundaries

Pursuing peace does not mean pretending that harm never happened. Wise boundaries can coexist with a sincere desire to avoid retaliation and pursue what is right.

Study: Romans 12:17–21 and Proverbs 4:23.

Love and Truth

Christian love is not the denial of truth. Speaking honestly, correcting wrongdoing appropriately, and treating people with dignity belong together.

Study: Ephesians 4:15 and 1 Corinthians 13:4–7.

Freedom and Personal Responsibility

Spiritual freedom is not permission to act without wisdom. It calls believers to let the truth of Jesus Christ shape their decisions, conduct, and treatment of others.

Study: John 8:31–36 and Galatians 5:1.
