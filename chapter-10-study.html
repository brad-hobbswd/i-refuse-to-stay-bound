Chapter 10 Study Guide

The Freedom of Setting Boundaries Without Guilt

Home›Inside the Chapters›Chapter 10›Study Guide
Group Study Guide

Group Discussion Questions

1. What is a biblical boundary?

Discuss how a healthy boundary differs from revenge, manipulation, avoidance, or an attempt to control another person’s behavior. Identify how truth, wisdom, love, and responsibility should shape a boundary.

2. Why can saying no create guilt?

Consider how family history, fear of rejection, church culture, or a desire for approval can influence a person’s ability to establish reasonable limits. Discuss why guilt deserves examination but is not always proof of wrongdoing.

3. What can we learn from Jesus withdrawing to pray?

Read Luke 5:15–16. Discuss how Jesus remained compassionate toward people while also maintaining His relationship with the Father and following His purpose. How might this example challenge the belief that faithful people must always be available?

4. How do forgiveness, trust, and access differ?

Give examples of situations in which forgiveness may be genuine even though trust needs time to rebuild or a relationship requires different limits. Explain why accountability and safety remain important.

5. How can the Church handle boundaries responsibly?

Discuss the responsibility of leaders and members to listen, communicate honestly, protect vulnerable people, investigate credible concerns fairly, and avoid retaliation. How can a congregation distinguish a legitimate concern from gossip or a careless accusation?

6. What is the difference between a boundary and a wall?

Discuss how healthy boundaries make room for trustworthy relationships while protecting against harmful patterns. Consider how fear can cause a person either to abandon necessary limits or to reject healthy relationships altogether.

7. Where might you need greater clarity?

Identify one area of life where expectations, commitments, or relationship patterns have become unhealthy. What honest conversation, reasonable limit, or request for support might help address the situation?

Personal Application

Take time to reflect honestly on the relationships and responsibilities in your life. Are you saying yes because you genuinely believe the commitment is right, or because you are afraid of what someone will think if you decline? Are you accepting repeated disrespect because you believe forgiveness requires unlimited access? Have you established a boundary to protect your well being, or have you withdrawn from someone in the hope that your silence will make them suffer?

Do not use these questions to condemn yourself. Use them to invite the Lord into the places where fear, guilt, resentment, and responsibility have become difficult to distinguish. Ask Him to show you where you need greater courage, where you need greater humility, and where you need the support of trustworthy people.

Remember that you do not have to resolve every relationship today. Some situations require time, careful planning, pastoral counsel, professional support, or formal intervention. If you are facing abuse, threats, coercion, or serious misconduct, prioritize safety and seek appropriate help rather than placing yourself at risk to demonstrate forgiveness.

Write down one boundary you believe may be necessary. Describe what it is intended to protect, how you can communicate it respectfully, and what action you will take if the harmful behavior continues. Then consider whether the boundary is proportionate to the situation and whether your response reflects the kind of person you are seeking to become in Christ.

During the coming week, practice one act of honest stewardship. You might decline an additional commitment because it would prevent you from meeting an existing responsibility. You might calmly communicate that a conversation cannot continue while insults are being used. You might seek guidance about a troubling situation instead of continuing to carry it alone. Or you might begin rebuilding a healthy relationship by communicating a reasonable expectation rather than assuming the other person should already understand it.

Before acting, pray for wisdom and examine your motives. Do not use the boundary to punish, embarrass, or control another person. Communicate as clearly as the circumstances allow, and remember that you cannot determine how the other person will respond. You can determine whether you will act with integrity.

At the end of the week, reflect on what you learned. Did you experience guilt or discomfort? What did those feelings reveal? Was the boundary respected, challenged, or misunderstood? Is there anything you need to clarify or adjust? Seek growth rather than perfection, and remember that learning to live with wisdom often requires practice.

Closing Prayer

Lord Jesus, teach me to love with a heart that is tender but not governed by fear. Help me recognize the difference between serving faithfully and abandoning the responsibilities You have entrusted to me. Forgive me for the times I have said yes dishonestly, allowed resentment to grow because I would not speak truth, or attempted to control others because I was afraid of being hurt again.

Give me wisdom to recognize healthy relationships, courage to establish appropriate boundaries, and humility to examine my own motives. Where I have used distance as punishment, teach me to repent. Where I have tolerated harm because I feared rejection, help me seek truth, safety, and wise support. Where trust has been damaged, show me what genuine accountability and responsible restoration require.

Teach Your Church to become a place where people can speak honestly, leaders can receive correction, vulnerable people are protected, and unity is built upon truth rather than silence. Help us honor one another without confusing spiritual authority with control or forgiveness with unlimited access.

Let my boundaries reflect wisdom rather than bitterness, conviction rather than pride, and love rather than fear. Help me pursue peace wherever it is possible without pretending that every relationship is safe or every conflict can be resolved by one person’s effort. Give me the grace to release revenge, the courage to take appropriate action, and the patience to allow trust to grow where it can be rebuilt.

May my life reflect Your character. Let me remain compassionate without becoming captive to the expectations of others, faithful without pretending to have unlimited strength, and open to love without abandoning truth. Teach me to guard my heart while continuing to walk in grace.

In the name of Jesus, Amen!
